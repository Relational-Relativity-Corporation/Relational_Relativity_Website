A mathematics of bounded observation

A mathematics of how a bounded observer relates to what they see.

From a sensory and cognitive perspective, a human being experiences a constantly changing, bounded domain of information. We are a relationship between an organism and its environment.

Metatron Dynamics has formalized that process mathematically, and is applying that formalization across substantially different declared domains.

The Perspective

What the operators attempt to model.

The framework's starting point is simple to state: the only mathematical primitive it uses is observable difference, relative to a declared boundary. Not a domain-specific model of biology, or materials, or energy — an attempt to model how a bounded observer relates one observable thing to another. Time is not required to define the primitive within the framework. Temporal change enters downstream rather than as a starting assumption.

Because that primitive doesn't reference any particular domain, the same declaration process — declare a boundary, declare what's observable within it, declare the relations that hold between those observables — is applied whether the observables are hydrogen bonds in a protein, material flows through a recovery system, or coordination between perception and motor output. Which operators are combined, and how, is determined by the declared regime of each analysis; the operators themselves are documented at abr-kernel →

The primitive is falsifiable in principle: an empirical datum whose informational content required no difference, distinction, or relation to a reference would challenge it directly.

The framework also requires that a declaration have a genuine Origin — a person, external to the system being evaluated, who declares the boundary and what's observable within it. Under the framework's provenance rule, a model cannot generate its own declaration and simultaneously serve as its Origin; that provenance would be circular.
